The formula, sources & fine print

NutriScore = Σ(concentration index × signed gene weight × SNP factor × pathway weight − allergen penalty − cooking penalty). Overall match blends a scaled Nutri-Score with a cuisine flavor rating. This custom score is unrelated to the front-of-pack Nutri-Score food label.

Cell chemistry means methylation: chemical processes involved in many cell functions. Antioxidant pathways relate to the body's response to reactive molecules. Fat processing means lipid metabolism. These are simplified labels, not measurements of your body's activity.

Compound quantities, gene weights, SNP modifiers, flavor ratings, and cooking losses are illustrative assumptions. No score has been clinically validated. The first slider moves from methylation through antioxidant to lipid emphasis, with a 10% minimum for each pathway.

Sources: MTHFR and folate: PMID 25788000; Laboratory COMT inhibition: PMID 15100171; APOE and dietary fat: PMID 25332476. These studies provide background, not proof that a recipe will benefit an individual.

Network support labels include indirect hypotheses, not necessarily direct activation of a gene. Line thickness follows the modeled contribution. The radar uses a fixed 0–100 demonstration scale; negative totals clip to zero. Recipe scores and charts adjust contributions to the listed serving amounts. The indices themselves remain synthetic.

48 ingredients, population-level and laboratory background only. Each ingredient expands to show its calculation and evidence status. Cooking methods change both preparation and an assumed loss fraction; none of these fractions is experimentally validated. No individual outcome prediction or assessment of nutritional adequacy. USDA energy metadata is separate from the synthetic compound indices; new seasonings have nutrient metadata pending. Cannot replace a clinician or registered dietitian. Nut filtering does not assess cross-contact or other allergies; check labels for soy, wheat, fish, and other ingredients.
